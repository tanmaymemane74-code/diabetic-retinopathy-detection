# American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Care in Diabetes (2024): Retinopathy Recommendations

## 1. Initial Screening Timelines
- **Type 1 Diabetes**: Adults and youth aged ≥ 10 years should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination within 5 years after diabetes onset.
- **Type 2 Diabetes**: Patients should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist AT THE TIME OF DIAGNOSIS, as asymptomatic retinopathy may already be present due to delayed diagnosis.
- **Pregnancy**: Women with preexisting type 1 or type 2 diabetes who are planning pregnancy or who become pregnant should be counseled on the risk of development and/or progression of diabetic retinopathy. Rapid glycemic tightening during pregnancy can temporarily accelerate retinopathy.

## 2. Examination Frequency
- If there is no evidence of retinopathy for one or more annual eye exams and glycemia is well controlled, screening examinations every 1 to 2 years may be considered.
- If any level of diabetic retinopathy is present, subsequent dilated retinal examinations should be repeated at least annually.
- Examinations are required more frequently (every 3–6 months) if retinopathy is progressing or sight-threatening lesions are detected.

## 3. Telemedicine and AI-Assisted Retinal Screening
- Retinal photography with remote reading or validated artificial intelligence (AI) systems can be an effective screening strategy for diabetic retinopathy.
- AI systems should be used as screening tools to identify patients who require timely specialist referral, NOT as autonomous diagnostic systems.
- Programs using retinal photography must ensure that referral pathways are established for prompt face-to-face clinical evaluation and therapy.

## 4. Systemic Risk Factor Modification
- Intensive diabetes management with the goal of achieving near-normoglycemic levels delays the onset and slows the progression of diabetic retinopathy.
- Blood pressure control reduces the risk of retinopathy progression.
- Lipid-lowering therapy with statins and/or fenofibrate may slow the progression of retinopathy, particularly in patients with baseline retinal exudates.
