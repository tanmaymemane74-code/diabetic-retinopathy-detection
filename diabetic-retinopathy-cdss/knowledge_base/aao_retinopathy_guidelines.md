# American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) Preferred Practice Pattern: Diabetic Retinopathy (2023)

## 1. Classification and Grading Criteria
The International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy Disease Severity Scale classifies diabetic retinopathy (DR) into five distinct stages based on funduscopy or retinal photography:

- **Grade 0: No Apparent Retinopathy**: No visible retinal abnormalities or microvascular lesions.
- **Grade 1: Mild Nonproliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (NPDR)**: Characterized exclusively by the presence of microaneurysms. No hemorrhages, hard exudates, or cotton-wool spots are present.
- **Grade 2: Moderate NPDR**: Presence of microaneurysms and more than just microaneurysms, but less than Severe NPDR. Includes dot-and-blot hemorrhages in 1 to 3 quadrants, hard exudates (lipid deposits), and cotton-wool spots (nerve fiber layer infarcts).
- **Grade 3: Severe NPDR (The 4-2-1 Rule)**: Marked by any one of the following criteria in the absence of proliferative retinopathy:
  1. Severe intraretinal hemorrhages and microaneurysms in each of 4 quadrants;
  2. Definite venous beading in 2 or more quadrants;
  3. Prominent intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA) in 1 or more quadrants.
  High risk of rapid progression to proliferative disease within 1 year (~50% risk).
- **Grade 4: Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (PDR)**: Defined by neovascularization of the disc (NVD), neovascularization elsewhere (NVE), preretinal or vitreous hemorrhage, or fibrovascular proliferation. High risk of severe vision loss.

## 2. Referral Considerations and Screening Guidelines
- **No DR (Grade 0)**: Annual dilated eye examination by an optometrist or ophthalmologist. Optimize glycemic control (target HbA1c < 7.0%), blood pressure (< 130/80 mmHg), and serum lipids.
- **Mild NPDR (Grade 1)**: Follow-up examination in 6 to 12 months. Reinforce systemic glycemic and hypertensive management.
- **Moderate NPDR (Grade 2)**: Refer to general ophthalmologist or retina specialist within 3 to 6 months. Closer surveillance is indicated if diabetic macular edema is suspected.
- **Severe NPDR (Grade 3)**: Urgent referral to a retina specialist within 2 to 4 weeks. High risk of progression to proliferative retinopathy; consider panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) or anti-VEGF therapy evaluation.
- **Proliferative DR (Grade 4)**: Immediate/prompt referral to a retina specialist within 24 to 48 hours to 1 week. Urgent treatment evaluation (anti-VEGF injections, panretinal laser photocoagulation, or vitrectomy).

## 3. Ungradable and Poor Quality Images
Any fundus image that cannot be reliably graded due to media opacity, cataract, poor dilation, or camera artifact MUST NOT be classified as disease-free. A patient with an ungradable photograph must be referred for an in-person dilated ophthalmic slit-lamp examination.
